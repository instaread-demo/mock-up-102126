Nick Khan’s Brutal Boxing Warning Haunts Zuffa as Stevenson Quits

  • by Phil Jay
  • October 21, 2026October 21, 2026 • • @PhilJWBN • @WorldBoxingNews •

Nick Khan’s brutal assessment of boxing’s failures over the last 20 years has come back to haunt Zuffa after Shakur Stevenson walked away without throwing a single punch.

The TKO president recently criticized boxing’s established promoters for letting major broadcasters down through slow decisions, poor communication and a failure to deliver. Now the operation backed by his own company has lost one of its biggest signings.

Speaking on The Varsity podcast, Khan revealed what network executives had told him about dealing with boxing promoters.

“Decisions that should take two hours take two weeks, and you can’t get straightforward answers from your partners.”

Khan insisted broadcasters still wanted boxing’s biggest fights, but had been let down by those responsible for delivering them.

“They like it. They want the big prize fights, but ultimately, the partners that they chose — they chose, pardon me — under-delivered for them.”

Zuffa Loses Shakur Stevenson

On October 19, WBN reported that Stevenson had secured his release after spending eight weeks preparing for fights that never materialized.

The four-division world champion signed with Dana White’s operation in July but left just 102 days later.

Stevenson made clear he was not blaming anyone, although Zuffa had lost one of its biggest names before putting him in the ring.

The unbeaten American had been linked to Devin Haney, while a proposed January clash with Jose Valenzuela also failed to materialize.

Negotiations involving Haney had their own complications, and there is no evidence Zuffa was solely responsible for those talks collapsing.

The $15 Million Question

Stevenson’s departure also exposed the gulf between what he wanted to earn and what Zuffa was reportedly offering.

As WBN reported, Stevenson was reportedly offered around $7 million to face Valenzuela after seeking $15 million per fight.

Conor Benn, meanwhile, reportedly secured $15 million for his original one-fight Zuffa agreement.

TKO president Mark Shapiro previously explained that Saudi partner Sela funded Benn’s purse rather than TKO itself, meaning the two deals were not directly comparable.

Zuffa managed to sign a four-division world champion but could not keep him long enough to stage a fight.

Zuffa’s Sanctioning Body U-Turn

Dana White’s words have constantly led many to believe Zuffa was about to take over boxing. However, the promotion has already reversed course on policies, including its original stance against sanctioning bodies.

White initially outlined plans to move away from the WBA, WBC, IBF and WBO, replacing their influence with Zuffa’s own titles and rankings.

That approach ran into problems with established champions, including Jai Opetaia, who wanted to continue pursuing recognized world championships.

White subsequently opened the door to working with existing sanctioning organizations where necessary to make fights happen.

The change may have been unavoidable, but it was a considerable U-turn from the original plan to challenge boxing’s established system.

White’s Deadline Approaches

Despite White’s ambitious plans, Zuffa has yet to assemble the kind of elite roster needed to challenge boxing’s established promoters.

There have been major signings, including Stevenson, Opetaia and Benn. One has gone, one has been knocked out badly, and White still needs to deliver the major fights he promised.

Losing a pound-for-pound star so publicly, without staging a single bout, is hardly the advertisement Zuffa wanted for its approach.

Khan wants boxing promoters to make decisions faster, answer their phones and deliver the fights broadcasters are paying for.

Zuffa has a major Paramount agreement and must meet those same standards.

White has already asked the boxing world to judge his operation by its body of work heading into 2027.

Khan’s criticism of boxing’s old guard may well be justified. But if Zuffa wants to replace the established order, it must prove it can avoid the very failures its own president has condemned.
